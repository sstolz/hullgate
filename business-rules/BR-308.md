# BR-308 · Unknown credentials stop writing beyond a budget

## Rule

When a key has presented 30 unknown credentials in the last 60 seconds, a further one gets the answer it would have had, unchanged, and writes no entry of its own; every presented unknown credential counts, written or not.

## Clarifications

- Unknown credential, closed list: at the access point the reasons `missing_token` and `invalid_token`; at `token`, `invalid_client`, `unsupported_grant_type`, and any refusal whose form presents no value, in the grant type's own field (`code` or `refresh_token`), that matches a stored row — absent, empty, single or multiple values alike. A client identifier is public: a refusal it alone carries proves no secret and counts as unknown. A refusal with an authentic credential is always written, with its actor: `revoked`, `node_deleted`, `audience_mismatch`, `out_of_scope`, `sensitive_requires_role_token`, `insufficient_scope`, reuse detection, and any refusal at `token` whose form presents at least one value matching a stored code or refresh token, whatever the answer (`invalid_target`, `invalid_grant`, a malformed multi-value form included). An attacker cannot hide it by spending the key first, nor by burying it among unknown values.
- BR-055 stays whole: same status, same `WWW-Authenticate`, so a third party on a shared key cannot hold the discovery signal away from a legitimate client.
- This reconsiders F-005 line 84. The aim is not guessing (256-bit tokens): it is the unbounded audit writes, hence no status change.
- Every request counts, an exception to BR-305: otherwise a sustained flood would fall back under budget each minute and write 30 rows a minute forever.
- A 404 is not counted (§Out of Scope).
