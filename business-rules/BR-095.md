# BR-095 · `revoquer` cuts access without losing the placements

## Rule

`hullgate connection revoke` revokes the tokens at the provider (best effort), erases the encrypted tokens and sets the connection to `réauthentification requise`, keeping its name, its prefix and its placements.

## Clarifications

- N3 b. `remove` (BR-075) then deletes. The failure of the revocation at the provider is audited (BR-066 v2), without blocking.
- `revoke` takes the lease of BR-089: it waits for the end of a renewal in progress, reads the **current** tokens, erases them, changes the state and increments `generation` in the same transaction, then revokes at the provider outside the transaction. A renewal result that arrives afterwards is discarded and revoked on a best-effort basis (BR-089): no revoked connection comes back to life.
- The revocation at the provider uses the kept `revocation_endpoint` (BR-097); if not announced, it is skipped and audited "unsupported". Without a key (BR-088), local erasure, revocation skipped and audited "key_unavailable".
- `revoke` is outside the lock (BR-049: a revocation always remains possible).
- An access token already returned to #9 stays valid at the provider until its deadline, unless the revocation reaches it (§12).
- A revoked connection no longer announces or calls any tool (F-008, BR-109, BR-113): its list is discarded at the next request, warm cache included, and a call is refused without an exchange; a call already gone out completes (BR-113).
