# BR-107 · Every upstream MCP call is bounded

## Rule

Every exchange of the relay with an upstream has a hard deadline on the whole exchange (name resolution included) of 15 s for the handshake and `tools/list` and of 60 s for `tools/call`, at most 1 MiB read (stream included), no redirect followed, no environment variable read (proxy, certificate store), all addresses checked before the connection and tried in the resolver's order with fallback, and as the only outgoing headers, **besides the two framing headers that HTTP/1.1 requires (`Host`, equal to the normalized host of the record's URL, and `Content-Length`, the size of the body sent)**, `Authorization` (the connection's token), `Content-Type`, `Accept`, `MCP-Protocol-Version`, `Mcp-Session-Id` (the one the upstream gave), a fixed `User-Agent` and, in generation 2026-07-28, `Mcp-Method`, `Mcp-Name` (for `tools/call`) and one `Mcp-Param-<nom>` per argument annotated `x-mcp-header` in the tool's upstream schema, these last three taken from the body sent to the upstream and never from a client header; a gap in the bounds ends the exchange and is classified according to BR-116 or BR-117.

## Clarifications

- Same mechanism as BR-084 (checked address tried, name used for the SNI and the certificate, `http.client`/`socket`/`ssl`), with a bounds profile of its own; the choice between extending `hullgate.outbound` and an SDK client is the implementation's under this rule (ADR expected, H12): any option that reads the environment, follows a redirect or does not try the checked address is excluded.
- The bounds of 15 s, 60 s, 1 MiB are technical ceilings of v1 (H6); the response ceiling **per role** belongs to #10 and can only be lower. No configuration option lifts them; the tests inject their clock, their resolver and their `dial`, a single test playing the real 60 s value.
- No header of the incoming client (cookie, `Origin`, `X-Forwarded-*`, the client's `Authorization`) is forwarded; no response header of the upstream is forwarded to the client (`Set-Cookie`, `WWW-Authenticate`, `Mcp-Session-Id`).
- An upstream response is a JSON or an SSE stream, read under the same bounds (handshake and `tools/list` included).
- The 1 MiB ceiling applies to the response **body** (stream included), not to its headers. The response headers are bounded by the hard deadline of the exchange (15 s or 60 s) and by the ceilings of `http.client` (at most 100 lines of 64 KiB); they do not count in the 1 MiB (operator decision, 2026-10-31, n1 of review 449).
- The value of a connection's token that contains a control byte (CR, LF, NUL) is refused before sending: `internal_error`, no exchange, audited event.
- `Mcp-Method`, `Mcp-Name` and `Mcp-Param-*` (post-freeze amendment of 2026-10-30, operator decision): required by a 2026-07-28 server (`mcp==2.3.0` otherwise rejects the request, `HEADER_MISMATCH`). `Mcp-Method` is the body's method, `Mcp-Name` the `name` of the `tools/call`, `Mcp-Param-<nom>` the value of the annotated argument (`true`/`false` for a boolean, text otherwise); a value outside visible ASCII, or bordered by spaces, is encoded `=?base64?…?=`; no final value contains CR, LF or NUL (same refusal as the token). A schema whose annotations are invalid emits no `Mcp-Param-*`, like a conforming client. An annotated argument that cannot be encoded as a header (a value that is neither text, nor number, nor boolean, or a header of more than 8 KiB once encoded) makes the call refused before any upstream `tools/call` with `request_too_large`: the client's request does not fit the form the upstream requires; it is neither a transient outage (`upstream_unavailable` would invite replaying it identically) nor a failure of the gateway (`internal_error`).
- A `Mcp-Session-Id` returned by the upstream is subjected to the same check (visible ASCII 0x21-0x7E, which the protocol requires) before being sent back in a header: otherwise the exchange is an `upstream_unavailable`, audited with the reason "session", no session held.
- `Host` and `Content-Length` are not application headers: HTTP/1.1 requires them to route and frame the request. `Host` carries the host of the record's URL, never the tried address nor a client's value; `Content-Length` the size of the body; no other transport header (`Accept-Encoding`, `Connection`, `Transfer-Encoding`, `Expect`, `TE`) is emitted (post-freeze amendment of 2026-10-30, operator decision, B1 of review 448).
- `http://` is admitted only for the host named by BR-106 and only if **all** its resolved addresses are private; a resolution that changes between the declaration and the call and leaves the policy (in particular a public address over `http://`) is refused at the call, without connecting.
